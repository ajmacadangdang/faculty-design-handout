Start with one clear idea

The way information is organized and presented can either support understanding or add unnecessary mental effort. You do not need to redesign an entire course. Start with one slide, one Canvas page, or one learning resource.

Before: many competing details

When everything appears equally important, learners may have trouble identifying the main message.

After: a clear learning message

One idea→Meaningful visual→Apply

An intentional sequence helps learners see the main idea and connect it with an example or application.

The five design moves

Use these research-informed multimedia design strategies when creating slides, Canvas content, study guides, or learning modules.

1 · Remove

Coherence

Remove unrelated words, images, and sounds that do not help students achieve the learning goal.

Example: Replace a crowded history slide with the three causes students must compare.

Ask: What can I remove without losing meaning?

2 · Highlight

Signaling

Use headings, labels, arrows, or emphasis to show what deserves attention.

Example: Add a clear annotation to the turning point in a graph.

Ask: Where should students look first?

3 · Connect

Spatial contiguity

Place explanatory words close to the corresponding parts of a visual.

Example: Put cell-structure names next to the structures rather than in a separate legend.

Ask: Can students see which label belongs to which part?

4 · Chunk

Segmenting

Break a complex explanation into manageable parts. Let students move through the parts at an appropriate pace when possible.

Example: Explain photosynthesis in stages rather than displaying every process at once.

Ask: Can I show one meaningful step at a time?

5 · Visualize

Multimedia

Pair relevant words with meaningful pictures or diagrams when the combination supports understanding.

Example: Use a labeled supply-chain diagram instead of several paragraphs describing the process.

Ask: Would a visual explain a relationship more clearly?

These are practical descriptions of selected principles, not an exhaustive list of Mayer's multimedia learning principles. The design move "Visualize" does not mean adding a picture to every slide.

Faculty multimedia design toolkit

Start with the tool you already have. Choose one that makes your instructional idea clearer—not one that adds extra work.

PowerPoint

Improve slides and create diagrams

Try: Design Suggestions (if available), SmartArt, built-in icons, charts, and alignment tools.

Design moves: Remove, Highlight, Connect, Chunk.

DesignPLUS in Canvas

Organize Canvas lessons

Try: Reusable content blocks, headings, and consistent page layouts.

Design moves: Remove, Highlight, Chunk.

PlayPosit

Make lecture videos more active

Try: Embedded questions, pauses, and explanatory feedback.

Design moves: Chunk; connect practice to key ideas.

Adobe Express

Make a quick teaching graphic

Try: Infographic templates, simple visual assets, and drag-and-drop layouts.

Design moves: Visualize, Highlight, Remove. Confirm your feature/license access.

Clipchamp

Shorten and organize recordings

Try: Trim unnecessary sections and arrange shorter clips. Check caption quality.

Design moves: Remove, Chunk. Features vary by access.

Pressbooks + H5P

Build interactive study resources

Try: An interactive diagram or a short self-check in an open learning resource.

Design moves: Connect, Visualize, Chunk. Confirm authoring access.

Fastest approach: In PowerPoint, try a built-in diagram first. In Canvas, start with a clear DesignPLUS layout. For video, use PlayPosit to ask one meaningful question at the right moment. UF availability and individual features may vary—check the UF Tool Finder.
